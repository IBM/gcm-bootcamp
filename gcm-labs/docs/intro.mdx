# Introduction to IBM's PQC Labs

This series of PQC labs is designed to walk you through IBM's post-quantum cryptography (PQC) strategy and approach for migrating your agency's classical cryptography to quantum-safe standards. This lab scenario mirrors what many federal agencies are facing: mission-critical workloads that must prepare for quantum threats like Harvest-Now, Decrypt-Later (HNDL) while maintaining operations and meeting federal PQC mandates.

The IBM Quantum-Safe Readiness Index (QSRI) measures an organization's readiness to adopt quantum-safe cryptography across people, processes, and technology. IBM's 2025 research found even the most advanced organizations score no higher than 35–50 out of 100. [Learn more about QSRI →](https://www.ibm.com/thought-leadership/institute-business-value/en-us/report/quantum-safe) The IBM labs are structured to help federal agencies begin now, discover and inventory their CBOM, assess their risks, create a plan, sequence work correctly, provide hands-on experience with software tools to build crypto-agility, and understand the resources and skills to execute their plan.

## Overview of the PQC Labs

### Lab 1. Discover and Inventory CBOM

Quantum Safe Explorer (QSE) integrates with GCM to analyze application code for cryptographic vulnerabilities. In Lab 1, you'll see how developers and SecOps collaborate to identify issues, fix them in code, and validate changes through CI/CD pipelines.

**Quantum Safe Explorer:** Automatically scans applications and infrastructure to inventory cryptographic assets and identify quantum-vulnerable algorithms.

### Lab 2. Analysis / Policy & Management / Migration

In Lab 2, you will use GCM's network scanner to discover quantum-vulnerable certificates, use AI-powered risk assessment to understand exploitability, renew and deploy a stronger replacement certificate using Certificate Lifecycle Management (CLM) integrated with HashiCorp Vault, and manage database encryption keys using GCM's TDE Key Management capability. These tasks demonstrate how GCM reduces risk and enforces cryptographic policies across an agency's full asset inventory.

**Guardium Cryptography Manager:** Prioritizes remediation/migration based on business impact and regulatory/mandate requirements, helping teams focus on high-risk areas.

**HashiCorp Vault (an integrated partner solution):** Acts as a centralized Certificate Authority and secrets management solution to issue CA-signed certificates, store keys, and control access to sensitive cryptographic material.

### Lab 3. Adaptive Remediation for Legacy Apps

During your PQC transition, your agency will encounter inconsistent cryptographic standards across applications and services — exposing sensitive data in User-to-Agency and Agency-to-Agency communications across multiple network boundaries.

In Lab 3, you will learn how to protect legacy applications using IBM Quantum Safe Remediator's (QSR) adaptive proxy — without modifying the applications themselves. You will deploy a Quantum Safe Reverse Proxy at the backend or cloud perimeter to handle incoming PQC traffic while preserving compatibility with legacy systems. You will deploy a Quantum Safe Forward Proxy on the client side to upgrade legacy TLS traffic to post-quantum before it crosses the internet.

**Quantum Safe Remediator:** Supports the transition to quantum-safe algorithms and policies, enabling crypto agility at scale.

## Architecture

Federal agencies and regulated enterprises face a unique challenge: cryptographic assets are embedded invisibly across sprawling hybrid environments — on-premises data centers, cloud workloads, containerized applications, legacy mainframes, OT systems, and interconnected supply chains — yet must all be migrated to quantum-safe standards within binding regulatory timelines. IBM's PQC architecture addresses this challenge through four integrated capabilities: (1) IBM Quantum Safe Explorer (QSE), (2) IBM Guardium Cryptography Manager (GCM), (3) IBM Quantum Safe Remediator (QSR), and (4) HashiCorp Vault — delivering an end-to-end cryptographic lifecycle — from discovery and risk-based prioritization through validated quantum-safe remediation — structured to meet NSM-10, CNSA 2.0, NIST FIPS 203/204/205, FISMA, and FedRAMP compliance mandates.
