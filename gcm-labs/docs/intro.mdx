# Introduction into IBM's PQC Labs

These series of PQC labs are designed to walk you through IBM's post-quantum cryptography (PQC) strategy and approach for migrating/remediating your agency's classic cryptography to be Post-Quantum Safe (PQS). This lab scenario mirrors what many of you (federal agencies) are facing: a federal agency with mission-critical workloads that must prepare for quantum threats like harvest now decrypt later (HNDL), supply chain and vendor dependency, etc. while maintaining operations and meeting federal PQC mandates.

The IBM Quantum-Safe Readiness Index (2025) found even the most advanced organizations score no higher than 35–50 out of 100. The purpose of these IBM's labs is to help federal agencies increase their QSRI score. The IBM labs are structure to help federal agencies begin now, discover/inventory their CBOM, assess their risks, create a plan, sequence work correctly, provide hands-on experience with software tools to build crypto-agility, and understand the resources/skills to execute their plan.

## Overview of the PQC Labs

### Lab 1. Discover and Inventory CBOM

Quantum Safe Explorer (QSE) integrates with GCM to analyze application code for cryptographic vulnerabilities. In lab 1, you'll see how developers and SecOps collaborate to identify issues, fix them in code, and validate changes through CI/CD pipelines.

**Quantum Safe Explorer:** Automatically scans applications and infrastructure to inventory cryptographic assets and identify quantum-vulnerable algorithms.

### Lab 2. Analysis / Policy & Management / Migration

In lab 2, you will use the core capabilities of Guardium Cryptography Manager (GCM). You'll learn how GCM provides visibility into cryptographic assets, supports compliance, and enables lifecycle management — all critical for building a Quantum Safe (QS) strategy. Inventory management is key to maintaining crypto-agility. Here, you'll run scans, review discovered assets and perform lifecycle operations like renewing and deploying certificates using IBM (Hashicorp) Vault. These tasks demonstrate how GCM helps reduce risk and enforce cryptographic policies.

**Guardium Cryptography Manager:** Prioritizes remediation/migration based on business impact and regulatory/mandate requirements, helping teams focus on high-risk areas.

**IBM (Hashicorp) Vault:** Acts as a centralized secrets management solution to secure, store, and control access to sensitive data like API keys, credentials, and certificates.

### Lab 3. Adaptive Remediation for Legacy Apps

During your PQC transition, your agency will come across inconsistent cryptographic standards across applications/services – exposing sensitive data in User-to-Agency and Agency-to-Agency communications. This can create potential security risks over multiple network boundaries and the internet.

In lab 3, you will learn how to assess PQC posture, verify cryptographic algorithms, and protect applications using IBM's Quantum Safe Remediator's (QSR) adaptive proxy. You will learn how to deploy QS Reverse Proxies at the backend or cloud perimeter to securely handle incoming PQC traffic, ensuring seamless interoperability with legacy applications while protecting network boundaries.

You will deploy QS Forward Proxy at the client side to upgrade legacy TLS traffic to Post-Quantum (PQ) before transmission over the internet.

**Quantum Safe Remediator:** Supports the transition to quantum-safe algorithms and policies, enabling crypto agility at scale.

## Architecture

Federal agencies and regulated enterprises face a unique challenge: cryptographic assets are embedded invisibly across sprawling hybrid environments — on-premises data centers, cloud workloads, containerized applications, legacy mainframes, OT systems, and interconnected supply chains — yet must all be migrated to quantum-safe standards within binding regulatory timelines. IBM's PQC architecture addresses this challenge through four integrated software capabilities: (1) IBM Quantum Safe Explorer (QSE), (2) IBM Guardium Cryptography Manager (GCM), (3) IBM Quantum Safe Remediator (QSR), and (4) IBM (HashiCorp) Vault to deliver an end-to-end cryptographic lifecycle — from discovery and risk-based prioritization through validated quantum-safe remediation — structured to meet NSM-10, CNSA 2.0, NIST FIPS 203/204/205, FISMA, and FedRAMP compliance mandates.
